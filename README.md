# ARKS Arcade

A self-hosted family and party game arcade: word games, card games, trivia, chess and more, served from one Node.js + Express + SQLite container. No external database, no ads, no sign-ups; it runs fine with no internet at all.

`/` is the arcade hub; each game lives on its own page.

## The games

### Wordless (`/wordless.html`)

A themed word-guessing game, Wordle-style.

- Guess a themed word in 6 tries, at Easy (3-4 letters) up to Ludicrous (9-10)
- Every puzzle has a category title; 3 hints climb from a clue sentence, to a word that ends the same way, to a letter. Clues are hand-written for the built-in themes, generated by the theme prompt for new ones (or added later from admin), and come from WordNet for Classic (`words/clues-common.tsv`)
- Arcade-style scoring (guesses, hints, and speed all factor in), computed server-side
- A shared, persistent leaderboard — per theme, and an "All Themes" combined view
- Kids submit theme ideas from the game; you get notified on Discord and fulfill them from an admin panel

### Rambler (`/rambler.html`)

A dice-and-grid word hunt against the clock. (Boggle-style — "Boggle" is a Hasbro trademark, hence the different name.)

- Rolls the **real printed dice**: the 16-die 4x4 set and the 25-die 5x5 Big set, Qu tile included. Boards are re-rolled until they hold enough words to be worth playing.
- Standard scoring (3–4 letters = 1, 5 = 2, 6 = 3, 7 = 5, 8+ = 11); 5x5 boards require four letters.
- **Duplicate cancellation** — a word more than one player found scores nothing for anyone, which is the rule that makes hunting obscure words pay.
- End-of-round reveal showing every word nobody found.
- Four ways to play:
  - **Solo** — you against the clock.
  - **Pass & Play** — one phone, 2–8 players, same board each turn.
  - **Tabletop** — one shared timer, everyone writes on paper, then the lists get typed in for scoring. The phone is just the buzzer and the scorekeeper.
  - **Own Phones** — a four-letter room code, everyone on their own device, synchronized timer, live word counts (counts only — never the words).
- Two dictionaries: a **Kids** list with the rude words removed, and an **Unfiltered** list with slang and profanity added on top. Separate leaderboards, so the two never share a scale.
- **Difficulty** — Easy (three-letter words count, roomier timer, richer boards), Normal (the box rules), Hard (four letters minimum, less time, scores multiplied by 1.35).
- **Timers up to ten minutes.** Three is the box rule and it is genuinely miserable for a seven-year-old.

#### Marathon

The mode that isn't in the box. You start on a small board with a short clock, and **every word you find buys you more time** — three seconds for a short one, twenty-five for an eight-letter monster. Find enough and **the grid grows a row or column** on a random side, opening letters that weren't reachable a moment ago. You never win; you last.

Because of Marathon, boards are rectangular internally. Anything that takes a board accepts `{ cols, rows }`, and a plain number still means a square.

#### Gamification

- **Profiles** — one per person, with an avatar, picked from a "Who's playing?" screen. Not accounts: no password, no email.
- **Streaks** — play on consecutive days and it climbs. Rolls over at *local* midnight, which is why `TZ` matters in the compose file.
- **Daily challenge** — one board a day, the same sixteen letters for everyone in the house, generated from the date rather than stored. Four minutes, fixed, so the standings mean something. One *scoring* go each: you can replay it as often as you like and nothing more is banked, which beats telling a seven-year-old no. Because it comes from the date, it is reproducible anywhere and survives losing the database entirely.

  The dictionary is the one thing that is not shared — the unfiltered list is wider, so an adult genuinely has more to find. The standings tag each row with the list it was played on rather than pretending the two are the same.
- **Milestones** — sixteen of them, from "find your first word" to "play thirty days running". Each pays coins.
- **Theme packs** — a pack of words about a subject (Halloween, Minecraft, whatever) that a season can wear. Its words become **valid** so `ENDERMAN` scores, they are **flagged** so finding one fires a celebration and pays **double**, boards are **re-rolled** until they actually contain some, and the accent color changes to match. Written by pasting a generated prompt into any chatbot and pasting the answer back — no API key, no cost.
- **Seasons** — the arcade boards run on a schedule you set (monthly by default, or any interval, or manual). When a season ends every board is frozen into **badges** and then cleared, so everyone starts level. A badge records the year, season, which board, the rank and the score. Streaks, coins, milestones and lifetime bests are **never** reset — only the walls are. Players get a countdown in the last few days.
- **The arcade board** — three walls (4x4, 5x5, Marathon), ten slots each. You only get to type your initials by beating the tenth score, and when you do, somebody is knocked off for good. Your profile keeps your personal best either way, so missing the wall costs you nothing but the wall.
- **Coins and hints** — earned slowly, spent on a hint that reveals a word still sitting on the board. A hinted word buys you time but scores nothing, so coins can't be converted straight into points.

#### A note on theme packs and word length

Measured on 400 random 4x4 boards, a theme word is reachable on:

| Word length | Boards where it can be traced |
|---|---|
| 3–4 letters | about 23% |
| 5–6 letters | about 0.3% |
| 7+ letters | essentially never |

That is geometry, not tuning — an eight-letter word needs a specific eight-cell
path. So a pack of long character names will look wonderful in the admin list
and never once appear in play. The generated prompt demands that two thirds of
the words be 3–5 letters, and the ingest reports the pack's measured
reachability so you can send it back for shorter words.

#### The Rambler API

Everything lives under `/api/rambler`.

| Route | What it does |
|---|---|
| `GET /config` | Sizes, timers, difficulties, milestone catalog, dictionary stats |
| `POST /board` | Roll a board (`size`, `profile`, `difficulty`) |
| `POST /check` | Validate one word against a board |
| `POST /score` | Score a finished round; pass `playerId` to bank progress |
| `POST /hint` | Spend coins to reveal a word |
| `GET/POST /players`, `GET/PATCH/DELETE /players/:id` | Profiles |
| `GET /daily`, `GET /daily/standings` | The daily board and its table |
| `POST /marathon`, `POST /marathon/:id/word`, `.../hint`, `.../finish` | A Marathon run |

Multi-device rooms use Server-Sent Events, which are built into Node and the browser — no websocket dependency. Rooms are in-memory and vanish on restart; only finished scores are persisted.

### A note on the dictionaries

ENABLE1 is a Scrabble tournament word list, so out of the box it already accepts essentially every swear word there is. That means the kids' blocklist is the load-bearing part, not the adult supplement.

Edit these by hand — they are plain text, one word per line, `#` for comments:

| File | What it does |
|---|---|
| `words/enable1.txt` | The base list (~172,000 words, public domain). |
| `words/blocklist-kids.txt` | Base words removed from the Kids dictionary. Inflections are generated, so listing `wank` also blocks `wanks`/`wanked`/`wanker`. |
| `words/allowlist-kids.txt` | Exact words the blocklist must never catch. Exists because inflecting stems produces false positives — `heroin` generates `HEROINES`, `bonk` generates `BONKERS`. |
| `words/supplement-adult.txt` | Words added to the Unfiltered dictionary that ENABLE1 lacks: American slang, anything coined recently. |
| `words/british.txt` | British spellings (COLOUR, CENTRE, ORGANISE…) and British slang. The arcade is American English: these are stripped from the other lists and refused on load, so theme packs and admin edits can't add them back. |
| `words/common-10k.txt` | Frequency list used only to decide which words are "common" enough to show a child on the reveal screen. |

Validation always uses the full list, so a real word is never rejected as fake — only the blocked ones are. Slurs aimed at race, sexuality, or disability are in neither list.

### Spin & Solve (`/spinsolve.html`)

A wheel-and-phrase puzzle game that follows the TV show's turns. On your turn you spin, buy a vowel for $250, or solve. Landing on money means calling a consonant, worth that much for every time it's in the puzzle, and finding one means you go again. A letter that isn't there, a wrong answer, Bankrupt (which also takes the round's money) or Lose a Turn passes the turn. Only whoever solves banks the round's money, at least $1,000. Solo gets three strikes a round and goes on the high-score board; 2-4 players pass one phone. 200+ built-in family-friendly puzzles in `public/spin-puzzles.js`.

- **The turn timer** (30 to 90 seconds, or off) only runs when you owe an answer, like on the show: once the wheel stops (to call a letter), when you buy a vowel, and when you tap Solve. Nothing ticks while you decide what to do next, so it never runs before your first spin.
- **Sounds** are our own, made in the browser like the rest of the arcade's (`public/blip.js`), in the spirit of a TV wheel game without using any of the show's recordings: a bright ding for each letter that lights up, a low buzzer for a letter that isn't there, a wrong answer or running out of time, a click for every peg the wheel passes, a sting for Bankrupt and a fanfare when the puzzle is solved.
- **Sound off:** each of those has something to see too. Letters glow as they turn over, the buzzer flashes the board red with a big ✕ over the wheel, the pointer kicks at every peg, and Bankrupt, Lose a Turn, Time's up and Solved show over the wheel.

Levels run Kids, Cheeky, Spicy, Filthy like the party card games; the three adult levels have their own built-in banks (`spinsolve/puzzles.js`), sit behind the adult PIN, switch on the adult look, and keep their own unannounced high-score board. Players can suggest a theme from the game; the admin builds packs at any level in the Spin & Solve tab by copying a prompt and pasting the AI's answer, and every pasted puzzle is checked to fit the board and use American spelling.

### Chess (`/chess.html`)

Against the computer (Easy, Medium, Hard), two players on one phone, or two phones with a room code. The rules engine (`public/chess-engine.js`) runs in the page, in a Web Worker for the computer, and on the server to check two-phone moves; the tests check it against the standard perft counts. Beating the computer scores on the high-score board.

### Dino Dash (`/dino.html`)

An endless runner: jump cactuses, duck birds, and it gets faster (and then dark). Tap or Space to jump, hold Duck or ↓ to duck. Original pixel art drawn from text bitmaps; all sounds synthesized.

### Snake (`/snake.html`)

Classic Snake on a 24x24 board, gliding smoothly between squares: Classic (walls end it) or Wrap-around, three speeds that score ×1/×2/×3, and a golden star every fifth apple that vanishes if you're slow. Swipe, the on-screen arrows, or arrow keys/WASD. Rules in `public/snake-engine.js`.

### Brick Breaker (`/bricks.html`)

Paddle-and-ball brick smashing: eight hand-made levels (looping faster), bricks that take one to three hits plus unbreakable gold, and power-ups (wide paddle, three balls, slow ball, fireball, extra life). A finger or mouse moves the paddle. The ball moves in small steps so it can't tunnel through bricks; the tests let an autopilot clear every level. Rules and physics in `public/bricks-engine.js`.

### Block Drop (`/blocks.html`)

Falling four-square pieces in a 10x20 well, with standard rotation and wall kicks, a shuffled bag of all seven pieces, hold, ghost piece, lock delay, combos, T-spins and back-to-back bonuses. Marathon (endless, faster every 10 rows) or a 2-Minute Rush. Tap to turn, drag to move, pull down to speed up (keep holding a moment to drop all the way; the ↓ key and Down button work the same), swipe up to hold, or use the buttons/keyboard. Rules in `public/blocks-engine.js`.

### Tower Defense (`/towers.html`)

Six maps from easy to hard (Meadow, Frozen Lake, Canyon, Swamp, Castle, Volcano; Swamp and Volcano have two roads), four towers with two upgrades each (Archer, Cannon, Frost, Tesla), five monsters including flying bats, armored knights and an ogre boss every fifth wave, and 25 waves to win. Waves come on their own: wave 1 arrives 30 seconds after your first tower, then there's a 15-second break after each wave. Calling the next wave early pays bonus gold, more the earlier you call it. Rules in `public/towers-engine.js`; the difficulty was tuned by letting bots of different skill play every map.

The four share `public/game-shell.js` (game-over panel, saving scores, high-score boards, personal bests) and `public/game-shell.css`. Each has its own soft background music, generated in the browser like the sound effects (`public/blip.js`): the 🎵 button turns it off and on, the browser remembers the choice, and 🔊/🔇 mutes everything. It stops while the game is paused or over.

### Disco Drop (`/discodrop.html`)

An arcade cabinet game for one player. A disco ball hangs from an arm at the top of the cabinet, and a row of cups slides past underneath it, each worth a different number of points. Tap to let go, and the ball falls straight down into whichever cup is under it when it gets there. The narrower the cup, the more it's worth: wide ones give 10 or 25, narrower ones 50 or 100, two dark BUST cups give nothing, and one tiny gold cup is the 500-point jackpot.

- **Ten balls a run, and it gets harder as you go.** The cups speed up after every ball and can change direction from the fourth ball on, the arm starts sliding back and forth on the fourth ball, the cups shuffle into a new order after balls 2, 5 and 7, and the last three balls score double (so the jackpot is worth 1,000 there). The number on each cup is always what it gives right now.
- **The server decides every score.** Starting a run picks a random seed on the server and writes down the moment it started. Everything that moves comes from that seed and the moments the balls were dropped, using whole-number math in one shared file (`public/discodrop-engine.js`), so the page and the server always agree. For each ball the page sends only *when* it let go, in milliseconds since the run started. The server replays the whole run from the seed to find where every ball landed and adds up the score itself. It turns away a drop that couldn't have happened: one before the last ball had landed and the next was back on the arm (a ball takes 0.65 seconds to fall and 0.35 to come back), one later than the server's own clock says the run has been going, or one that arrives more than ten seconds after it happened. A score sent by the page is ignored.
- **Celebrations are earned.** A jackpot gets a banner, a coin shower and a jingle, but only when the server says the ball landed in it. "So close!" only shows when the server measured the ball landing right next to the jackpot cup.
- **Boards** for today and for all time. Every run you save counts. Profiles show their name or initials (their own choice), and guests type initials. A new best-ever run posts to the Comm Center.
- **Phones first, and TV-ready:** the whole cabinet is the tap area, with a big DROP button under it, and it fits a phone screen with no sideways scrolling. **Space** or **Enter** drops on a keyboard, and a game controller's **A**, **X**, **R1** or right trigger works through the browser's Gamepad API. The animation runs on `requestAnimationFrame`, and with reduced motion turned on the lights, spotlights and sparkle hold still (the cups still move, since that's the game).

### Moonshot (`/moonshot.html`)

An arcade batting cage for one player. You stand at the plate under the stadium lights, and a pitching machine throws twelve pitches at you, one at a time. Tap to swing just as the ball reaches the glowing ring over the plate. The closer your timing, the bigger the hit, and a perfect swing sends the ball over the wall and up to the moon for a home run.

- **Timing is everything.** The cage measures how far your swing was from the moment the ball reached the ring, in milliseconds (thousandths of a second). Within 25 either way is a home run (100 points), then a triple (60) within 50, a double (40) within 80, a single (25) within 120, a grounder (5) within 160 and a foul ball (2) within 210. Anything further off, or no swing at all, is a strike. Early swings go to the left and late ones to the right, and after every swing the cage says how many milliseconds early or late it was.
- **Three pitches, and they speed up.** A fastball is quick and straight, a changeup leaves the machine the same way but comes in slower, and a curveball swings out to one side and bends back over the plate. The lights around the machine fill up during its windup, so you can see each pitch coming. Pitches 1 to 4 score normal points, 5 to 8 come faster and score double, and 9 to 12 come faster still and score triple. Home runs in a row earn a streak bonus of 50 points for each one before it (up to 200 extra, before the double or triple). A run takes about a minute, and the most it can score is 6,700.
- **The server decides every score.** Starting a run picks a random seed on the server and writes down the moment it started. Every pitch comes from that seed alone, using whole-number math in one shared file (`public/moonshot-engine.js`), so the page and the server always agree. For each swing the page sends only *when* it happened, in milliseconds since the run started. The server replays the whole run from the seed to work out every hit and adds up the score itself. It turns away a swing that couldn't have happened: between pitches, a second one at the same pitch, one later than the server's own clock says the run has been going, or one that arrives more than ten seconds after it happened. A score sent by the page is ignored.
- **Pausing is fair.** Opening How to play, the high scores or another tab pauses the run, on the page and on the server, so the clocks stay together. Coming back gives you a short countdown before the next ball.
- **Celebrations are earned.** A home run gets a banner, a coin shower, fireworks over the moon and a jingle, but only when the server says it was one. "So close!" only shows when the server measured a swing within 15 milliseconds of the home-run window, and it says by how much.
- **Boards** for today and for all time. Every run you save counts. Profiles show their name or initials (their own choice), and guests type initials. A new best-ever run posts to the Comm Center.
- **Phones first, and TV-ready:** the whole cage is the tap area, with a big SWING button under it, and it fits a phone screen with no sideways scrolling. **Space** or **Enter** swings on a keyboard (**P** or **Esc** pauses), and a game controller's **A**, **X**, **R1** or right trigger swings through the browser's Gamepad API (**Start** pauses). The animation runs on `requestAnimationFrame`, and with reduced motion turned on the stars, lights and fireworks hold still (the pitches still move, since that's the game).

### Sudoku (`/sudoku.html`)

The classic 9x9 number puzzle: fill every empty square with 1 to 9 so no row, column or 3x3 box has the same number twice. There's a new puzzle at midnight on each of four levels, and it's the same puzzle for everyone that day (it's made from the date alone), so the family can race each other.

- **Levels, graded by how a person would solve them.** The server tries each puzzle with the tricks people use and keeps it only if it fits its level. **Family** starts with at least 46 numbers and every square can be found by spotting the one number it's missing, so it's good for kids. **Easy** starts with about 36 and also needs "this number can only go in one place in this row, column or box". **Medium** (about 27 to 30) gets stuck on those and needs pencil-mark tricks (pairs, and a number pinned to one line of a box) to finish. **Hard** is one those tricks can't finish. Every puzzle has exactly one answer.
- **Pencil marks.** The Notes button puts small numbers in a square as reminders; placing a real number rubs it out of the notes around it.
- **Made on the server, with no outside library** (`sudoku/generator.js`): a seeded random full grid, then numbers taken away in mirrored pairs while a solver checks there's still only one answer. A whole day's puzzles take a few milliseconds and are kept in memory.
- **The server keeps the time.** Start records the moment on the server and hands the page a long random run id and the puzzle, never the answer. When the board is full the page sends the grid; the server checks it against the answer and works the time out itself, leaving out pauses (Pause, or leaving the page, stops the clock and hides the board). A finish quicker than 0.4 seconds per square is turned away.
- **Help costs time, decided by the server:** a hint fills in (or fixes) one square for 30 seconds, Check marks every wrong number for 15 seconds, and the "Show my mistakes as I go" setting (off to start with) adds 10 seconds for each wrong number it catches.
- **Fastest-time boards** for today and for all time, per level. Only a player's first finish of the day counts, because a second go at the same puzzle would be easy. Profiles show their name or initials (their own choice, as on every board), and guests type initials. A profile has one clock per puzzle, and its filled-in squares and pencil marks are saved on the arcade too (a moment after you stop tapping, and when you leave the page), so opening the puzzle on another device carries on with the same time and the same board. Guests keep their progress on their own device. Saved progress is only ever handed back to the page: the finish still checks the whole grid it's sent. A new fastest-ever time posts to the Comm Center.
- **Phones first:** the board fills the width, a row of nine big number buttons sits under it, and the keyboard works too (arrows, 1-9, Delete, N for notes, P to pause).

### Shuffle Scuffle (`/scuffle.html`)

A word race. Everyone gets the same 6 or 7 letters, mixed up, and races to find the word they spell (the **full word**). Every smaller word you can make from the letters, 3 letters or more, scores too, so there's always something to find while you hunt for the big one. A round lasts 60, 75 or 90 seconds, and a game is several rounds, starting with six letters and moving up to seven.

- **Points by length:** 10 for 3 letters, 20 for 4, 35 for 5, 60 for 6 and 90 for 7, plus 100 for the full word. Any word that uses every letter counts as the full word (LISTEN and SILENT both do). In a room, the first person to find it gets 50 more.
- **Daily:** four rounds of 75 seconds, with letters made from the date alone, so everyone gets the same ones that day. The first go of the day can go on the daily high-score board (today and all time). The full words are always everyday words on the kids list; the word list you pick only decides which smaller words count, and each row on the board says which list it was played with. A profile gets one daily a day.
- **Free play:** new letters every game, 3, 5 or 7 rounds, any round length. These scores don't go on a board.
- **Rooms, a phone each:** the host gets a four-letter code (and a QR code), picks the rounds, the time and the word list, and everyone races on the same letters at once. Each phone sees how many words the others have found and a star when someone gets the full word, but not their words until the round ends. Latecomers can join mid-game, and rooms show on the Join a game board.
- **Family words or unfiltered:** the family list is the kids dictionary (`words/blocklist-kids.txt` keeps rude words out). Unfiltered is the full list and follows the arcade's adult rules: the adult PIN (or an ARKS ID adult pass) to play it, and a lobby password the host makes up for a room. Guests get in with the password, never the PIN. Unfiltered rounds sometimes hide a cheeky word from `words/spice-adult.txt`.
- **The server decides everything** (`scuffle/`): it picks and mixes the letters, starts each round's clock, checks every word (in the letters, on the chosen list, not already found) and adds up every score. The page sends only the words people spell; a score or time in the request is ignored, words after the clock (plus a second and a half for the network) are refused, and a round's words are only sent once it's over.
- **Phones first:** big letter tiles to tap, Shuffle, Back, Clear and Enter, the words you've found, and a countdown. On a keyboard you can just type. After each round you see the full word and every word you missed.

### Words with Family (`/crisscross.html`)

A crossword tile game for 2 to 4 players. Everyone has seven letter tiles, and you take turns laying them on an 11 by 11 board to make words that cross the words already there. It's built for playing on everyone's own phone over a few days: a turn can wait hours, and the arcade's home page says "Your turn in N games" on the Words with Family card.

It was called Criss Cross until November 2026. Only the name changed: the page is still `crisscross.html`, the API is still `/api/crisscross`, and the tables and saved games are the same, so old links, bookmarks and games in progress keep working. The logo (`public/words-with-family-logo.svg`) is our own: wooden letter tiles spelling the name, each with the point value it has in this game.

- **Each person on their own phone** needs a profile (or ARKS ID), because a game lasts days and has to know who you are. **New game with the family** lists the family's profiles to tick (up to three), and can leave seats open for a share link or QR code that anyone with a profile can join. A game with only invited people starts straight away; one with open seats starts when they fill up, or when the host taps Start with whoever has joined. The game list shows whose turn it is, the last move and how long ago it was, and an open game checks for new moves every few seconds.
- **On this phone** is pass-and-play, open to guests: two to four names, and the computer can take any seat. Between turns the tiles are hidden behind a "Pass the phone to Sam" screen. Only that device can open the game (it keeps a key).
- **The rules:** the first word covers the star in the middle. After that, a turn's tiles go in one line, across or down, with no gaps, joined onto tiles already on the board, and every new word made (crosses included) has to be on the game's word list. Two-letter words count. Instead of playing you can swap tiles (when 7 or more are left in the bag), pass, or leave. The game ends when the bag is empty and someone uses their last tile (they get everyone else's leftover points, which everyone else loses), or after six scoreless turns in a row (everyone loses their leftovers), or when only one player is left.
- **Points:** our own 66-tile bag and values: common letters are worth 1, and Q and Z are worth 10. Two blanks can be any letter and score nothing. The board's bonus squares double or triple a letter (2L, 3L) or a word (2W, 3W, and the star in the middle), on the turn a tile lands on them. Using all seven tiles in one turn adds 40.
- **Words:** family words (the kids list, `words/blocklist-kids.txt` keeps rude words out) or unfiltered. An unfiltered game follows the adult rules for every player, not just the host: each request needs the adult PIN (or an ARKS ID adult pass), and with ARKS ID on, every invited profile has to belong to a grown-up's account.
- **The server decides everything** (`crisscross/`): it keeps the bag and every rack, shows each player only their own rack (everyone's leftovers are shown once the game is over), checks every move and finds every new word, and adds up the score. The page only sends where its tiles went; a score in the request is ignored.
- **The computer** (`crisscross/bot.js`) plays the best-scoring word it can find with its rack along every row and column. It doesn't use blanks, and when it can't find a word it swaps or passes.
- **Phones first:** tap a tile and then a square, or drag a tile onto the board; tap a placed tile to take it back. Shuffle, Recall, Swap and Pass, a Zoom button that makes the board bigger and lets it slide around inside its own box, the score of your word before you play it, the last move lit up, and a list of every word played. There's no chat between players, on purpose.
- **No push messages.** ARKS ID doesn't offer a way to send a short "your turn" note, so turns show up on the home page and the game list instead.

### Clueless (`/clueless.html`)

A small crossword every day, the same one for everybody (it's made from the date alone), so the family can race each other for the fastest time. The **Family mini** is a 5 by 5 grid with clues written for kids, and the **Grown-ups** crossword is a 7 by 7 grid with longer words and trickier clues. Both use words from the family word list. There's no unfiltered crossword, because there are no grown-up-only clue packs to build one from.

- **Playing:** tap a square and type; the word you're on lights up and its clue shows in the bar above the grid. Tap the same square again (or the clue bar) to switch between across and down, and tap any clue in the lists to jump to it. Phones get a keyboard on the page; a laptop's own keyboard works too (arrows, Space to switch direction, Tab for the next clue). **Pencil** puts letters in gray for the ones you're not sure of.
- **Where the clues come from:** every clue is written by a person or pasted in from a chatbot by the admin; the server never asks an AI for anything. The game ships with about a thousand family clues written for it (`clueless/starter-clues.txt`), and admin can add **crossword clue packs**: copy a prompt with a batch of words from the arcade's everyday list, paste it into any chatbot, and paste the `WORD: clue` lines back. Each clue is checked on the way in: the word must be on the family list (3 to 7 letters, no British spellings), the clue must be 80 characters or fewer, it can't contain its own answer (or a word that starts with it), and it can't use a word the kids blocklist removes. Family clues go in both crosswords; grown-up clues only in the bigger one.
- **Making the grid, in our own code** (`clueless/grid.js`): a few hand-made shapes of black squares per size, the same upside down, with every word 3 letters or more. The filler tries clued words in an order seeded by the date, always filling the spot with the fewest words that still fit next, and backs up when it gets stuck; no word appears twice. If the words can't fill one shape it tries the next, and if none work it falls back to the built-in clues alone, which fill every shape (the tests try a few years of dates). A day's puzzles are saved the first time anyone opens them, so they stay the same all day even if a clue pack is added or the server restarts.
- **The server keeps the answers and the time.** Start records the moment on the server and hands the page a long random run id, the shape and the clues, never a letter. **Check** (a square for 5 seconds, a word for 10, the whole puzzle for 20) says which letters are wrong without saying what's right, and **Reveal** (a square or a word, 15 seconds a letter) hands over only the letters asked for, and the server writes down every one. When the grid is full the page sends it, and the server checks it against the answer and works the time out itself, leaving out pauses. A finish quicker than 0.3 seconds for each square you typed is turned away.
- **Fastest-time boards** for today and for all time, for each crossword. Only a player's first finish of the day counts, and a profile has one clock per puzzle, so opening it again on another device carries on with the same time. A new fastest-ever time posts to the Comm Center.

### Tumbler Rumble (`/tumbler.html`)

A timing game in the style of a coin-op cabinet. A bright light races around a lock, and one spot on the ring glows gold: that's the **pin**. Tap the moment the light is on the pin to pop it, and the light turns around while a new pin lights up somewhere ahead of it straight away. A run never stops to start over: it goes on until the first time you tap when the light isn't on the pin, or let it slide past the pin without a tap, and your score is how many pins you popped.

- **A real countdown:** every run starts with 3, 2, 1 and Go on the lock, one second each. The POP! button says Start before a run, stays dimmed and can't be pressed during the countdown (it counts down too), and lights up when the light starts moving.
- **Waves:** the pins come in waves of 10. The first pins are wide and slow (130 degrees a second, with 16 degrees either side of a pin's middle counting as a hit). In each wave the light speeds up and the pin narrows for 7 pins, then eases off for the last 3, and each wave starts and peaks a bit harder than the one before. By about 110 pins the waves peak at the hardest there is (320 degrees a second, 9 degrees either side) and still ease off and climb back. The Pace box says whether the wave is getting harder or easier right now.
- **Bullseyes:** popping a pin in the middle third of it is a bullseye. They don't change the score, but the run's bullseyes and best streak show at the end.
- **Sound off:** every sound has something to see too. Pops, bullseyes, Go and each new wave make the lock glow, a miss turns it red (and shakes it, unless reduced motion is on), and wave news shows across the top of the lock.

- **Controls:** tap anywhere on the lock or the big POP! button on a phone, press Space or Enter on a keyboard, or press a game controller's A, X, R1 or right trigger (through the browser's Gamepad API; Start pauses). The lock is drawn on a canvas at the screen's frame rate. With reduced motion turned on, the glowing trail, the shake and most of the confetti are left out, and the game plays the same.
- **The server works out every score.** Starting a run writes down the moment on the server's clock and hands the page a random seed, which decides where every pin goes. The page and the server run the same small simulation (`public/tumbler-engine.js`): the page plays it live, and when the run ends it sends only the list of its tap times, in whole milliseconds since the run started. The server replays those taps through the simulation and gets the score itself. It refuses taps that couldn't have come from a real run: two taps closer together than 60 milliseconds, taps out of order, more than 4,000 of them, a tap after the run was already over, or times later than the run has really lasted by the server's clock. Opening How to play or leaving the page pauses the run, and there's a countdown before the light moves again.
- **High-score boards** for today and for all time, counted in pins popped. Every run you save counts, profiles show their name or initials the way they chose, and guests type initials. After a run the game says how many more pins would have passed the next real score on today's board. A new best score ever posts to the Comm Center. Scores from the first version of the game (points over locks that opened) are kept in the database, marked `rules = 1`, but left off the boards, since the two kinds of score can't be compared.

### Roll Royale (`/royale.html`)

A ball-rolling game in the style of a coin-op cabinet, like skee-ball with a power meter. You get **9 balls**, and each one rolls up a neon lane, hops off a lip and drops into a target board at the top. The board has five rings: **10, 20, 30 and 40** from the outside in, and **50** for the small one in the middle. Above the rings is a little gold **jackpot cup** worth 100. A ball that misses every ring scores nothing.

- **Aim, then power.** Before each ball an arrow sways left and right, with a dashed line showing exactly where the ball would go across the board. Pressing locks the aim. While you hold, the power meter climbs for a second, then comes back down if you keep holding, and letting go rolls the ball with the power the meter shows. The meter has a pink band for the power that reaches the middle ring and a gold band for the jackpot cup's row. A tap shorter than 80 milliseconds isn't a roll, and a ball held for 4 seconds rolls by itself.
- **Bonus balls:** the last three balls have a star on them. On those, the jackpot cup slides from side to side and is worth 200, and the arrow sways faster on later balls (a trip there and back takes 2.6 seconds on balls 1 to 3, 2.1 on balls 4 to 6 and 1.7 on balls 7 to 9). A perfect run is 1,200 points.
- **Controls:** press and hold anywhere on the lane or the big HOLD · ROLL button on a phone (letting go anywhere counts, so a flick up the lane works), hold Space or Enter on a keyboard, or hold a game controller's A, X, R1 or right trigger (through the browser's Gamepad API; Start pauses). The lane is drawn on a canvas at the screen's frame rate. With reduced motion turned on, the glow, the shake and the moving lights are left out, and the game plays the same.
- **The server works out every score.** Starting a run writes down the moment on the server's clock and hands the page a random seed, which decides where each ball's arrow starts its sway and where the bonus cup starts sliding. The page and the server run the same small simulation (`public/royale-engine.js`), with simple sums instead of a physics engine: the aim at the moment you pressed and the power at the moment you let go give the spot where the ball lands. When the ninth ball is done the page sends only the press and release times, in whole milliseconds since the run started, and the server replays them to find every landing spot and the score itself. It refuses times that couldn't have come from a real run: anything but exactly 9 balls, a ball pressed before it was ready, a press and release closer together than 80 milliseconds or further apart than 4 seconds, or times later than the run has really lasted by the server's clock. Opening How to play or leaving the page pauses the run (a ball you were holding goes back), and there's a countdown before it carries on.
- **High-score boards** for today and for all time. Every run you save counts, profiles show their name or initials the way they chose, and guests type initials. After a run the game says how many more points would have passed the next real score on today's board. A new best score ever posts to the Comm Center.

- The home page's Join a game board lists every hosted room in any game, with scan-to-join QR codes in each game's lobby and walkthrough. Adult rooms are listed too but locked: hosting one needs the adult PIN and a lobby password the host makes up; guests only need that password
- Spin & Solve, Chess and Dino Dash share one high-score table (`/api/scores/:game`); new #1s post to the Comm Center
- Feedback from anywhere: a tiny red bug sits in the bottom-right corner of every page. Tap it, then tap "Found a bug? Tell us." to open a card where you write what happened, pick the kind and the game, and add a screenshot (snap the page, or upload one) that you can draw on with a pen, a box or an arrow. The home page's Send feedback button and the `/feedback` cheat code open the same card. Messages land in the admin's Comm Center inbox, and ping Discord when `DISCORD_WEBHOOK_URL` is set. A picture can be up to 1.5 MB (the card shrinks screenshots to fit), and the arcade keeps up to 500 MB of them in all; past that, notes still arrive without their pictures and the admin gets one note about it. Someone without a profile can send 5 notes an hour from one device, a profile can send 30 in 3 minutes, and the admin has no limit
- Everything is American English, word lists included: `words/british.txt` is refused everywhere

## Deploying to a server

`docker-compose.prod.yml` in this repo is the only file the server needs — it
pulls the image CI built rather than building anything locally:

```bash
mkdir arks-arcade && cd arks-arcade
# copy docker-compose.prod.yml here as docker-compose.yml, and .env.example as .env
docker compose pull && docker compose up -d
docker compose ps          # STATUS should read "healthy"
```

The container runs as a non-root user, drops privileges, caps itself at 512MB
(the dictionary trie sits around 120MB resident), and reports unhealthy if the
word lists fail to load rather than merely if the port is open.

Set `TZ` — daily challenges and streaks roll over at local midnight.

## The compose file in full

Once the GitHub Actions workflow (`.github/workflows/docker-publish.yml`) has published an image to GHCR, deploy anywhere with just this file — no need to clone the repo:

Copy [`docker-compose.prod.yml`](docker-compose.prod.yml) next to your `.env` (save it as `docker-compose.yml`), then:

```bash
docker compose pull
docker compose up -d
```

Put this behind a reverse proxy (Nginx Proxy Manager, Caddy, Traefik) for HTTPS, and use Tailscale or WireGuard rather than opening ports. See [docs/SETUP.md](docs/SETUP.md).

## Profiles

Like streaming-service profiles for the family. **Who's playing?** on the home page lets you pick a profile, or play as a guest (type a name, or get "Guest 1", "Guest 2"…). **New profile** asks for one with a name and an avatar (Shibas, blocky, toons, movies, music, space and animals); an admin approves it. A 6-digit PIN on a profile is optional and can be added, changed or removed later by its owner. Scores, Rambler stats and player names in every game follow the profile, and the leaderboards show each profile's avatar.

## Admin panel

Visit `/admin.html` and enter your `ADMIN_PASSWORD`.

**Stats:**
- What gets played, how often and for how long (only on-screen, recently-touched time counts), who plays what, and a day-by-hour chart of when
- A log of every visit, filterable by game and player
- The error log: crashes from browsers and the server, grouped by a code like `E-3F9A2C` (players see the code when the server fails). **Copy for chat** copies everything needed to get it fixed

**Arcade:**
- Profiles: approve or turn down "New profile" requests, rename, clear a forgotten PIN, unlock a profile locked by wrong PINs, or remove a profile (their old scores stay on the boards)
- Set, change or remove the adult PIN, and unblock devices that tried too many wrong PINs
- Home page games: reorder them, hide them, or turn one off while it's being worked on (it stays on the home page, faded, under a "Pardon our dust" sign)
- Comm Center: the feedback inbox (with screenshots, the page and device each message came from, and a Copy for chat button), announcements, high-score boards, Spin & Solve and Know-It-All packs and requests

**Boards:**
- Take a single score off any game's board (Sudoku, Clueless, Tumbler Rumble, Disco Drop, Roll Royale, Moonshot, Shuffle Scuffle, Wordless, and the games that share the older high-score board). Pick the game and board, then tap Remove twice. It leaves the board and everyone's ranks straight away but isn't deleted: it's kept in a list under the board with when it was taken off and by whom, and Put back returns it exactly as it was. A game added later shows up here by itself, as long as its scores live in a table named `<game>_scores`. Rambler's boards keep their own tools on the Rambler tab.

**Rambler:**
- See the current season, how long is left, and rename it ("Spooky Season")
- Change the reset schedule — every N days/weeks/months, or manual only — and how many days out players get warned
- End a season immediately: freezes every board into badges, then clears them
- Moderate the boards: remove a single entry (for when a child puts something unrepeatable in their three letters) or clear a whole board
- Manage profiles: rename, gift coins, delete
- Hall of fame: every finished season and who won each board
- Theme packs: copy a prompt, paste the answer back, and dress the season in it. The ingest tells you what percentage of boards the pack's words will actually appear on, so you find out immediately if it is all nine-letter names
- Dictionary: add or remove words from the browser. Edits live in the database, not the word files, so they survive a `docker compose pull`

**Clueless:**
- Make crossword clue packs: pick Family or Grown-up clues, copy the prompt (it carries a batch of words that don't have a clue yet), paste the chatbot's `WORD: clue` lines back, and see which ones were kept and which were skipped and why
- See how many words have clues, and delete a pack

**Wordless:**
- See pending theme requests, with a one-click "Copy Prompt" to paste into an AI chatbot (or use however you like) to generate the theme's word list
- Paste the resulting JSON back in to create the theme and mark the request fulfilled
- Delete custom themes
